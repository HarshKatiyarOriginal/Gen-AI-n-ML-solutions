## Unit 1: Constitution & Fundamental Rights

#### Section A: 2-Mark Questions

### Q1. Objectives of the Directive Principles of State Policy. <span class="m">[2 Marks]</span>

**Ans.** The **DPSPs** are in **Part IV (Articles 36 to 51)**, borrowed from the **Irish** Constitution. They are **non-justiciable** (Article 37) but **fundamental in governance**. **Objectives:**
1. A **welfare state** with social and economic democracy (Articles 38, 39).
2. **Equal pay for equal work** (Article 39(d)) and **free legal aid** (Article 39A).
3. **Gandhian ideals:** panchayats (Article 40), prohibition (Article 47).
4. A **Uniform Civil Code** (Article 44), the **environment** (Article 48A), and **separation of the judiciary from the executive** (Article 50).

### Q2. Constitutionalism and the unitary bias of the Constitution. <span class="m">[2 Marks]</span>

**Ans.**
- **Constitutionalism** means **government power is limited by the constitution**, through the rule of law, separation of powers, judicial review and Fundamental Rights.
- **Unitary bias:** India is **quasi-federal** (K.C. Wheare), with a **strong Centre**:
  - A "Union of States" (Article 1).
  - Single citizenship.
  - Parliament can change state boundaries (Article 3).
  - Residuary powers lie with the Union (Article 248).
  - Emergency provisions.
  - Governors are appointed by the Centre.
  - All-India Services.

### Q3. What are Fundamental Duties? <span class="m">[2 Marks]</span>

**Ans.** **Moral duties of citizens** in **Part IV-A, Article 51A**.
- Added by the **42nd Amendment (1976)** on the recommendation of the **Swaran Singh Committee**. The idea comes from the **USSR**.
- There were 10; the **11th** (education for children aged 6 to 14) was added by the **86th Amendment (2002)**.
- Examples: respect the Constitution, the Flag and the Anthem; protect the environment; develop a scientific temper.
- They are **non-justiciable**.

#### Section B: 7-Mark Questions

### Q4. Historical background and formation of the Constituent Assembly. <span class="m">[7 Marks]</span>

**Ans.** The **Constituent Assembly** framed the Constitution of India. It was formed under the **Cabinet Mission Plan (1946)**, adopted the Constitution on **26 November 1949**, and the Constitution came into force on **26 January 1950**.

{{fig:c1:0}}

**Historical background:**
1. **Regulating Act 1773:** the first control over the East India Company.
2. **GoI Act 1858:** power passed to the **British Crown**.
3. **Morley-Minto Reforms 1909:** separate electorates.
4. **Montagu-Chelmsford 1919:** **dyarchy** in the provinces.
5. **GoI Act 1935:** federation and provincial autonomy. **Most of our Constitution is based on it.**
6. The demand for a Constituent Assembly was raised by **M.N. Roy (1934)** and by the **INC (1935)**. It was accepted in the **August Offer (1940)**.

**Formation:**
- **389 members** (296 from British India, 93 from the princely states). They were **indirectly elected** by the provincial assemblies, about 1 seat per 10 lakh people.
- After Partition, the number fell to **299**.

**Working:**
- **First meeting: 9 December 1946.** President: **Dr. Rajendra Prasad**.
- **Objectives Resolution:** moved by **Nehru** (13 December 1946); it became the Preamble.
- **Drafting Committee:** **Dr. B.R. Ambedkar** (29 August 1947). Adviser: **B.N. Rau**.
- It took **2 years, 11 months and 18 days**.
- The original Constitution had **395 Articles, 22 Parts and 8 Schedules**.

**Criticism:** not directly elected, dominated by the Congress, too lengthy.

**Conclusion:** the Assembly gave India the **world's longest written constitution**.

### Q5. Explain Fundamental Rights and how they differ from other legal rights. <span class="m">[7 Marks]</span>

**Ans.** **Fundamental Rights** are the **basic rights guaranteed in Part III (Articles 12 to 35)**, inspired by the **US Bill of Rights**. They are **justiciable**: a person can go **directly to the Supreme Court under Article 32**.

{{fig:c1:1}}

1. **Right to Equality (14 to 18):** equality before law, no discrimination, equal opportunity, **abolition of untouchability (17)**.
2. **Right to Freedom (19 to 22):**
   - **Article 19:** six freedoms.
   - **Article 21:** life and personal liberty.
   - **Article 21A:** education.
3. **Against Exploitation (23 and 24):** no forced labour; no child labour below 14.
4. **Freedom of Religion (25 to 28).**
5. **Cultural and Educational Rights (29 and 30):** for minorities.
6. **Constitutional Remedies (32):** the **five writs**: habeas corpus, mandamus, prohibition, certiorari, quo warranto. Ambedkar called it the "**heart and soul**" of the Constitution.

**Key points:**
- The Right to Property was removed by the **44th Amendment**. It is now a legal right under **Article 300A**.
- **Articles 20 and 21 cannot be suspended**, even during an emergency.
- FRs can be amended, but **not the basic structure** (Kesavananda Bharati, 1973).

| Fundamental Rights | Ordinary legal rights |
|---|---|
| Come from the **Constitution** | Come from **ordinary laws** |
| **Direct remedy** in the SC / HC (Articles 32, 226) | Through ordinary courts |
| Changed only by a **constitutional amendment** | Changed by an ordinary law |
| Mainly against the **State** | Mainly against individuals |
| Example: Article 21 | Example: Article 300A (property) |

**Conclusion:** Article 21 now includes the **right to privacy (Puttaswamy, 2017)**, a clean environment and livelihood.

### Q6. Discuss the Preamble of the Indian Constitution. <span class="m">[7 Marks]</span>

**Ans.** The **Preamble** is the **introduction** to the Constitution. It is based on Nehru's **Objectives Resolution**. It has been **amended only once**, by the **42nd Amendment (1976)**, which added **Socialist, Secular and Integrity**.

{{fig:c1:2}}

1. **"We, the people of India":** the **people are the source of authority**.
2. **Sovereign:** independent of any external power.
3. **Socialist:** democratic socialism, ending inequality.
4. **Secular:** no State religion; all religions treated equally.
5. **Democratic:** an elected government with adult franchise.
6. **Republic:** an **elected head of State**.
7. **Objectives:**
   - **Justice:** social, economic, political (from the **Russian Revolution**).
   - **Liberty:** of thought and expression.
   - **Equality:** of status and opportunity.
   - **Fraternity:** dignity and unity (from the **French Revolution**).

**Is it a part of the Constitution?**
- **Berubari (1960):** No.
- **Kesavananda Bharati (1973):** **Yes**. It can be amended, but not its basic structure.
- **LIC case (1995):** Yes.

> **Remember:** the Preamble is **non-justiciable**. N.A. Palkhivala called it the "**identity card**" of the Constitution.

**Conclusion:** the Preamble is the **soul of the Constitution** and guides its interpretation.

## Unit 2: Union & State Executive

#### Section A: 2-Mark Questions

### Q1. Define Union Executive and State Executive. <span class="m">[2 Marks]</span>

**Ans.**
- **Union Executive (Articles 52 to 78):** the **President** (nominal head), the Vice-President, the **PM and Council of Ministers** (real executive) and the Attorney General.
- **State Executive (Articles 153 to 167):** the **Governor** (nominal head), the **CM and Council of Ministers** (real executive) and the Advocate General.

### Q2. Explain Judicial Review. <span class="m">[2 Marks]</span>

**Ans.** **Judicial review** is the **power of the courts to declare laws and executive actions void if they violate the Constitution**.
- **Basis:** Articles 13, 32, 226. **Origin:** Marbury v. Madison (USA, 1803).
- It is part of the **basic structure** (Kesavananda 1973, Minerva Mills 1980), so it cannot be removed.

#### Section B: 7-Mark Questions

### Q3. Compare the powers of the President of India and the President of the USA. <span class="m">[7 Marks]</span>

**Ans.** India has a **parliamentary system** in which the **President is the nominal head**. The USA has a **presidential system** in which the **President is the real executive**.

{{fig:c2:0}}

| Basis | President of India | President of USA |
|---|---|---|
| Position | **Nominal** head | **Real** head of State and Government |
| Election | Indirect: elected MPs and MLAs (Articles 54, 55) | Electoral College (near-direct) |
| Term | 5 years, no limit | 4 years, **maximum 2 terms** |
| Advice of ministers | **Binding** (Article 74) | Not binding |
| Ministers | MPs | Not members of Congress |
| Dissolve the legislature | Lok Sabha (on the PM's advice) | Cannot dissolve Congress |
| Veto | Absolute, suspensive, pocket | Qualified (overridden by 2/3), pocket |
| Ordinance | **Yes** (Article 123) | No |
| Emergency | **Articles 352, 356, 360** | None |
| Removal | Impeachment for violation of the Constitution (Article 61), 2/3 of each House | House impeaches, **Senate (2/3)** convicts, for treason, bribery or high crimes |

**Conclusion:** the Indian President "**reigns but does not rule**". The US President both reigns and rules.

### Q4. Legislative and financial powers of the Lok Sabha. <span class="m">[7 Marks]</span>

**Ans.** The **Lok Sabha** (Article 81) is the **directly elected** House, with a **maximum of 550 members** (543 elected seats now) and a term of 5 years. It has **more power than the Rajya Sabha**, especially over **money**.

{{fig:c2:1}}

**Legislative powers:**
1. **Ordinary bills:** can be introduced in either House. A deadlock goes to a **joint sitting (Article 108)**, presided over by the **Speaker**. The Lok Sabha's numbers usually win.
2. **Amendments (Article 368):** equal power with the Rajya Sabha (no joint sitting).
3. Approves ordinances within **6 weeks**. Elects the Speaker, and takes part in the President's election.

**Financial powers:**
1. A **money bill (Article 110)** can be introduced **only in the Lok Sabha**.
   - The **Speaker certifies** it.
   - The Rajya Sabha can only **recommend within 14 days**.
2. **Budget (Article 112):** the **demands for grants are voted only by the Lok Sabha** (Article 113).
3. Passes the **Appropriation Bill (Article 114)** and the Finance Bill. Cut motions; vote on account.

**Control over the executive:**
- The **Council of Ministers is collectively responsible to the Lok Sabha** (Article 75(3)).
- A **no-confidence motion** can be moved only in the Lok Sabha.

| Power | Lok Sabha | Rajya Sabha |
|---|---|---|
| Money bill | **Introduces and passes** | Only recommends (14 days) |
| No-confidence motion | **Yes** | No |
| Joint sitting presided by | **Speaker** | - |

### Q5. Powers of the Prime Minister and the Chief Minister. <span class="m">[7 Marks]</span>

**Ans.** The **PM** (Articles 74, 75, 78) and the **CM** (Articles 163, 164, 167) are the **real executive heads** at the Union and State levels.

{{fig:c2:2}}

**Powers of the PM:**
1. **Council of Ministers:**
   - Recommends ministers and **allocates portfolios**.
   - Chairs the Cabinet.
   - **The PM's resignation dissolves the whole Council.**
2. **President:** is the **channel of communication (Article 78)**. Advises on key appointments.
3. **Parliament:** is the leader of the House. Advises **dissolution of the Lok Sabha**.
4. **Other:** **chairman of NITI Aayog**; leads foreign policy; crisis manager.

**Powers of the CM:** the same roles at the State level:
- Ministers and portfolios.
- **Channel to the Governor (Article 167).**
- Advises the dissolution of the Vidhan Sabha.
- Member of the NITI Aayog Governing Council and the Inter-State Council.

| PM | CM |
|---|---|
| Appointed by the President (Article 75) | Appointed by the Governor (Article 164) |
| Responsible to the Lok Sabha | Responsible to the Vidhan Sabha |
| Article 78 | Article 167 |

### Q6. Independence of the Supreme Court and the appointment of judges. <span class="m">[7 Marks]</span>

**Ans.** The **Supreme Court** (Articles 124 to 147) has **34 judges including the CJI**. It is the **guardian of the Constitution**. **Judicial independence is part of the basic structure.**

{{fig:c2:3}}

**Appointment:**
- **Article 124(2):** judges are appointed by the President after consultation.
- **Qualifications:** a High Court judge for 5 years, a High Court advocate for 10 years, or a distinguished jurist.
- **Judges cases:**
  - **First (1981):** the executive had primacy.
  - **Second (1993):** the **collegium system was created**.
  - **Third (1998):** the collegium became the **CJI + 4 senior-most judges**.
  - **NJAC (99th Amendment, 2014):** **struck down in 2015**.

**Safeguards for independence:**
1. **Tenure until 65.** Removal only by **special majority in both Houses** for proved misbehaviour or incapacity (Article 124(4)).
2. **Salaries charged on the Consolidated Fund**, and **not reducible** (except in a financial emergency).
3. **No practice after retirement** (Article 124(7)).
4. **Conduct not discussed** in Parliament (Article 121).
5. **Contempt power** (Article 129). Own staff (Article 146).
6. **Separation from the executive** (Article 50).

**Criticism:** the collegium is **opaque**; appointments are **delayed**; post-retirement posts may affect independence.

## Unit 3: Legal System & Workplace Law

#### Section A: 2-Mark Questions

### Q1. Key provisions of the RTI Act, 2005. <span class="m">[2 Marks]</span>

**Ans.** The RTI Act came into force on **12 October 2005**. Any **citizen** can ask a **public authority** for information.
1. **Section 4:** suo motu disclosure.
2. **Section 6:** apply to the **PIO** with a **Rs 10 fee** (no fee for BPL); **no reason needed**.
3. **Section 7:** reply within **30 days**, or **48 hours** for matters of life or liberty.
4. **Section 8:** exemptions (security, cabinet papers, personal information).
5. **Section 19:** first appeal, then a second appeal to the **Information Commission**.
6. **Section 20:** a **penalty of Rs 250 per day, up to Rs 25,000**.

#### Section B: 7-Mark Questions

### Q2. Structure of the Indian court system: District Courts to Supreme Court. <span class="m">[7 Marks]</span>

**Ans.** India has a **single, integrated, hierarchical judiciary**. Appeals go **upward**, and the **decisions of higher courts bind the lower courts** (Article 141).

{{fig:c3:0}}

1. **Supreme Court:**
   - **Original jurisdiction (Article 131):** Centre vs. State disputes.
   - **Writs (Article 32).**
   - **Appeals (Articles 132 to 136**, including the SLP).
   - **Advisory (Article 143).**
   - **Court of record (Article 129).**
2. **High Courts (25):**
   - **Writs (Article 226)**, which are **wider** than Article 32 (they also cover legal rights).
   - **Supervision of the lower courts (Article 227).**
   - Judges retire at 62.
3. **District and Sessions Judge (Article 233):** the main court of the district, appointed by the **Governor with the High Court**. A death sentence needs **High Court confirmation**.
4. **Civil side:** Civil Judge (Senior Division), then Civil Judge (Junior Division) / Munsif.
5. **Criminal side:** Chief Judicial Magistrate, then Judicial Magistrates.
6. **Others:** **Lok Adalats** (no fee; the award is final), Family Courts, Gram Nyayalayas, Tribunals (NCLT, NGT), Consumer Commissions.

| Supreme Court | High Court | District Court |
|---|---|---|
| Articles 124 to 147 | Articles 214 to 231 | Articles 233 to 237 |
| Retire at 65 | Retire at 62 | State rules |
| Writs under Article 32 | Writs under Article 226 | No writs |

**Conclusion:** e-Courts, NJDG and Lok Adalats are reducing the pendency of cases.

### Q3. Contract law and the essentials of a valid contract. <span class="m">[7 Marks]</span>

**Ans.** Contract law is governed by the **Indian Contract Act, 1872**. Under **Section 2(h)**, a **contract = an agreement enforceable by law**. "All contracts are agreements, but not all agreements are contracts."

{{fig:c3:1}}

**Essentials (Section 10):**
1. **Offer and acceptance:** the acceptance must be **absolute and communicated** (Sections 2(a), 2(b), 7).
2. **Intention to create legal relations:** social agreements do not count (*Balfour v. Balfour*).
3. **Lawful consideration:** "quid pro quo" (Sections 2(d), 25).
4. **Competent parties (Section 11):** a major, of sound mind, not disqualified. An agreement with a **minor is void** (*Mohori Bibee*, 1903).
5. **Free consent (Section 14):** no **coercion (15), undue influence (16), fraud (17), misrepresentation (18)** or mistake. Otherwise the contract is **voidable**.
6. **Lawful object (Section 23).**
7. **Not expressly void (Sections 24 to 30):** for example restraint of trade (27) or wagering (30).
8. **Certainty (Section 29)**, **possibility of performance (Section 56)**, and the **legal formalities** required by law.

| Void | Voidable | Illegal |
|---|---|---|
| Not enforceable at all | Enforceable at one party's option | Forbidden by law |
| Example: with a minor | Example: consent by coercion | Example: smuggling |

**Remedies:** damages (Section 73), specific performance, injunction.

### Q4. Employee rights, employer responsibilities and workplace law. <span class="m">[7 Marks]</span>

**Ans.** **Workplace law** governs the employer-employee relationship. India has **consolidated its labour laws into 4 Labour Codes (2019-2020)**, supported by Constitutional Articles 14 to 16, 21, 23, 24 and 39 to 43.

{{fig:c3:2}}

**Employee rights:**
1. **Fair wages:** minimum wages, timely payment, **equal pay** (Code on Wages, 2019).
2. **A safe workplace**, fixed hours, leave (OSH Code, 2020).
3. **Social security:** PF, ESI, **gratuity (after 5 years)**, **maternity leave of 26 weeks**.
4. **No harassment:** under the **POSH Act 2013** (*Vishaka* guidelines), an **Internal Committee** is required if there are 10 or more employees.
5. **No discrimination.** The right to form **unions** (Article 19(1)(c)). Due process before termination. Data privacy (DPDP Act 2023).

**Employer responsibilities:**
1. Issue an **appointment letter** with clear terms.
2. **Pay on time**, and deposit **PF, ESI and TDS**.
3. Provide a **safe workplace** and training.
4. Run a **POSH committee** and an equal-opportunity policy.
5. Maintain **records**; follow **due process** for discipline; protect employee data.

| Area | Employee right | Employer duty |
|---|---|---|
| Wages | Minimum, equal pay | Pay on time |
| Safety | Safe workplace | Safety measures |
| Dignity | No harassment | POSH committee |
| Security | PF, ESI, gratuity | Deposit contributions |

### Q5. Explain the Information Technology Act, 2000. <span class="m">[7 Marks]</span>

**Ans.** The **IT Act, 2000** is India's law on **e-commerce and cybercrime**. It is based on the **UNCITRAL Model Law (1996)**, came into force on 17 October 2000, and was **amended in 2008**.

{{fig:c3:3}}

**Objectives:** legal recognition of **e-records, e-contracts and digital signatures**; **e-governance**; punishment of **cybercrime**.

**Key sections:**
- **3:** digital signature. **3A:** electronic signature. **4:** e-records. **10A:** e-contracts.
- **Sections 17 to 34:** the **CCA** licenses the Certifying Authorities.
- **43:** compensation for damage to a computer. **43A:** failure to protect data.

| Section | Offence | Punishment |
|---|---|---|
| 65 | Tampering with source code | 3 years |
| 66 | Hacking (dishonest) | 3 years / Rs 5 lakh |
| 66C | **Identity theft** | 3 years + Rs 1 lakh |
| 66D | **Phishing / cheating by personation** | 3 years + Rs 1 lakh |
| 66E | Privacy violation | 3 years |
| 66F | **Cyber terrorism** | **Life imprisonment** |
| 67 | Obscene content | 3 to 5 years |

- **Section 66A** was **struck down** in *Shreya Singhal (2015)*.
- **69 / 69A:** interception and website blocking. **70B: CERT-In.** **79:** intermediary safe harbour.

**Conclusion:** the IT Act supports e-filing, online banking and Aadhaar e-Sign. Data protection is now covered by the **DPDP Act 2023**.

## Unit 4: Elections, Emergencies & Amendments

#### Section A: 2-Mark Questions

### Q1. Ordinary Bill vs. Constitutional Amendment Bill. <span class="m">[2 Marks]</span>

**Ans.**

| Ordinary Bill | Amendment Bill |
|---|---|
| Articles 107, 108 | **Article 368** |
| Simple majority | **Special majority** in each House |
| Joint sitting possible | **No joint sitting** |
| President may return it | President **must assent** (24th Amendment) |
| No State ratification | Half the States ratify federal provisions |

### Q2. When can a National Emergency be declared? Types of amendment under Article 368. <span class="m">[2 Marks]</span>

**Ans.**
- **National Emergency (Article 352):** for **war, external aggression or armed rebellion** ("armed rebellion" replaced "internal disturbance" through the 44th Amendment). It needs the Cabinet's **written** advice and **approval within 1 month by special majority**. Declared in **1962, 1971 and 1975**.
- **Types of amendment:**
  1. **Simple majority** (Articles 2 to 4, citizenship).
  2. **Special majority** (FRs, DPSPs).
  3. **Special majority + ratification by half the States** (federal provisions, Article 368 itself).

#### Section B: 7-Mark Questions

### Q3. Composition, powers and functions of the Election Commission of India. <span class="m">[7 Marks]</span>

**Ans.** The **ECI** is a **permanent, independent constitutional body under Article 324**, set up on **25 January 1950** (National Voters' Day). It conducts elections to **Parliament, the State Legislatures, and the offices of the President and Vice-President**.

{{fig:c4:0}}

**Composition:**
- **CEC + 2 Election Commissioners** (multi-member since 1993). Decisions by **majority**.
- **Appointment:** by the President, on a **selection committee's** recommendation (the PM, the Leader of the Opposition and a Union Minister), under the **2023 Act**.
- **Term:** **6 years or until age 65**.
- **Removal:** the **CEC like a Supreme Court judge**; the ECs only on the CEC's recommendation.

**Functions:**
1. **Administrative:**
   - **Electoral rolls**, voter ID (EPIC), the **election schedule**.
   - **Recognising parties and allotting symbols**.
   - EVM / VVPAT.
   - Expenditure limits and observers.
2. **Regulatory:** enforces the **Model Code of Conduct**; rules on exit polls and paid news.
3. **Quasi-judicial:**
   - Settles party and symbol disputes.
   - Advises the **President (Article 103)** and the **Governor (Article 192)** on **disqualification**.
   - Cancels polls and orders re-polls.
4. **Advisory:** recommends electoral reforms.

**Limitations:** the MCC is not statutory; the ECI cannot deregister parties; the 2023 committee has a government majority.

**Conclusion:** the ECI ran the 2024 elections with about **97 crore voters**.

### Q4. Three types of emergencies, focusing on President's Rule. <span class="m">[7 Marks]</span>

**Ans.** The **emergency provisions** are in **Part XVIII (Articles 352 to 360)**, borrowed from the **Weimar Constitution of Germany**. During an emergency, the federation becomes **almost unitary**.

{{fig:c4:1}}

1. **National Emergency (Article 352):** war, external aggression or armed rebellion. Approved within 1 month by **special majority**. FRs can be suspended (**except Articles 20 and 21**).
2. **President's Rule (Articles 356 and 365):**
   - **Grounds:** the President, on the **Governor's report or otherwise**, is satisfied that the **State government cannot be carried on according to the Constitution**. Article 365 covers a State that **does not follow Union directions**.
   - **Cases:** a hung assembly; loss of majority with no alternative; a breakdown of the constitutional machinery.
   - **Not valid grounds:** maladministration; no **floor test** held; political reasons.
   - **Procedure:** approval within **2 months by simple majority**. **6 months at a time, maximum 3 years.** Beyond 1 year, a National Emergency and the **EC's certificate** are needed (44th Amendment).
   - **Effect:** the President takes over the State executive; **Parliament makes the State's laws**; the High Court is unaffected.
   - **S.R. Bommai (1994):** **judicial review is allowed**; the **floor test is the only test of majority**; secularism is part of the basic structure.
   - First used in **Punjab (1951)**; used **more than 100 times** since.
3. **Financial Emergency (Article 360):** a threat to India's financial stability. **Salaries, including judges', can be reduced.** It has **never been declared**.

| Basis | Article 352 | Article 356 | Article 360 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Approval | 1 month, special majority | 2 months, simple majority | 2 months, simple majority |
| Maximum duration | No limit | **3 years** | No limit |
| Used | 3 times | 100+ times | Never |

**Conclusion:** the **44th Amendment and the Bommai judgment** now prevent misuse of these powers.

## Unit 5: Business Organizations & E-Governance

#### Section A: 2-Mark Questions

### Q1. What is a sole trader? Types of shares. <span class="m">[2 Marks]</span>

**Ans.**
- **Sole trader:** a business **owned and controlled by one person**, who takes all the profit and bears **unlimited liability**. It has **no separate legal entity** and is easy to form. Example: a kirana shop.
- **Shares (Section 43, Companies Act 2013):**
  - **Equity shares:** voting rights, dividend paid last, with or without DVR.
  - **Preference shares:** fixed dividend and priority. Types: cumulative, participating, convertible, **redeemable** (Section 55).
  - Also: **bonus (63), rights (62), sweat equity (54)** shares and ESOPs.

#### Section B: 7-Mark Questions

### Q2. Formation of a company under the Companies Act. <span class="m">[7 Marks]</span>

**Ans.** A **company** is an **artificial legal person** (Section 2(20), Companies Act 2013) with a **separate entity, limited liability and perpetual succession**. It is formed **online through SPICe+ on MCA21**.

{{fig:c5:0}}

1. **Promotion:**
   - **Promoters** (Section 2(69)) do the feasibility study, arrange capital and choose the type.
   - **Private company:** 2 members, 2 directors. **Public company:** 7 members, 3 directors. **OPC:** 1 member.
2. **DSC and DIN** for the directors.
3. **Name reservation:** SPICe+ Part A (RUN).
4. **Documents:**
   - **MOA (Section 4):** the charter, with the name, registered office, **objects**, liability and capital clauses. The company **cannot act beyond it (ultra vires)**.
   - **AOA (Section 5):** the internal rules.
5. **File SPICe+ Part B** with INC-33 / 34 (e-MOA / e-AOA), INC-8 and INC-9, the office proof and the fees. PAN, TAN, GST, EPFO and ESIC are obtained in the same form.
6. **Certificate of Incorporation (Section 7)** with the **CIN**, issued by the **ROC**. From this date the company is a **legal person**.
7. **Commencement of business (Section 10A):** file **INC-20A within 180 days**.

| Private | Public | OPC |
|---|---|---|
| 2 to 200 members | 7 or more members | 1 member |
| 2 directors | 3 directors | 1 director |
| "Pvt Ltd" | "Ltd" | "(OPC) Pvt Ltd" |

### Q3. Role of IT professionals and engineers in e-governance and the judiciary. <span class="m">[7 Marks]</span>

**Ans.** **E-governance** uses ICT to deliver government services (G2C, G2B, G2G). **IT professionals design, build, secure and run** these systems, including the **e-Courts** of the judiciary.

{{fig:c5:1}}

**In e-governance:**
1. **Build portals and apps:** UMANG, DigiLocker, **GeM**, CoWIN, Passport Seva.
2. **Digital public infrastructure:** **Aadhaar, UPI, e-KYC, e-Sign** (India Stack).
3. **Cybersecurity:** PKI, encryption, audits, **CERT-In**, compliance with the **DPDP Act 2023**.
4. **Data analytics and AI** for policy, and fraud checks in DBT.
5. **Networks and cloud:** NIC, SWAN, State Data Centres, MeghRaj, BharatNet.
6. **Accessibility:** multilingual tools (Bhashini) and Common Service Centres.

**In the judiciary:**
1. **e-Courts project:** CIS and the **NJDG** (case pendency data).

### Q4. Digital Signature Certificates and cyber regulations. <span class="m">[7 Marks]</span>

**Ans.** A **digital signature** authenticates a document using **asymmetric cryptography plus a hash** (Section 3 of the IT Act). A **DSC** binds a person's **identity to their public key**. It is issued by a **Certifying Authority** licensed by the **CCA**.

{{fig:c5:2}}

**Working:**
1. Hash the document to get a **digest**.
2. **Encrypt the digest with the private key.** This is the signature.
3. The receiver **decrypts it with the public key** (from the DSC) and hashes the document again.
4. **If the two digests match, the signature is valid.** This proves **authenticity, integrity and non-repudiation**.

**DSC:**
- **X.509** format: name, public key, validity, the CA's signature.
- **Class 3** (Class 2 was discontinued in 2021). Valid for **1 to 3 years**, on a **USB token**.
- **Uses:** MCA, GST, income tax, e-tender filings.
- **Section 5:** legally equal to a handwritten signature.

**Cyber regulations:**
1. **IT Act 2000 / 2008.**
2. **SPDI Rules 2011** (Section 43A).
3. **Intermediary Rules 2021.**
4. **CERT-In directions 2022:** report incidents **within 6 hours**.
5. **DPDP Act 2023:** consent, a Data Protection Board, penalties **up to Rs 250 crore**.
6. **BNS 2023 / BSA 2023** (criminal law and evidence).

| Digital signature | Electronic signature |
|---|---|
| PKI with a DSC from a CA | Any reliable method (OTP, Aadhaar e-Sign) |
| Section 3 | Section 3A |
| Detects any change to the document | Depends on the method |

**Conclusion:** DSCs are **mandatory for MCA and GST filings**. Cyber laws keep India's digital ecosystem safe.
