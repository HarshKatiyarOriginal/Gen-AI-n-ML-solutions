CHAPTER 01

The philosophy

Knowledge-Vault starts from one belief: every student deserves study material that matches their exact syllabus, is correct, and is pleasant to study from, whatever university or course they are in.

Today that material is made by hand, one subject at a time, so only popular subjects at big universities get good notes. We want a machine that does the work of an entire publishing house, so that the quality of the books our team made by hand becomes available for every subject.

PRINCIPLE 1

Syllabus first

Content follows the official syllabus: university → course → semester → subject → unit → topic. Nothing extra, nothing missing.

PRINCIPLE 2

Exam-focused

We teach what earns marks: ★ important topics, solved numericals in the exam pattern, and answers written in 2, 7 and 10-mark formats.

PRINCIPLE 3

Truth with sources

Every fact comes from a real source and is checked before it is published. The AI is not allowed to guess.

PRINCIPLE 4

Learn your way

The same knowledge in four forms: interactive e-book to understand, PDF to revise, flashcards to remember, quizzes to test.

PRINCIPLE 5

Any university

AKTU is the first market only. The engine reads any syllabus, so any university, course or language can be added.

PRINCIPLE 6

Affordable

Free to start, nominal prices for full content. Low cost is possible because the machine, not a person, does the writing.

Seedhi baat

Hum notes bechne wali company nahi hain. Hum woh machine bana rahe hain jo kisi bhi syllabus ke best notes khud bana de.

CHAPTER 03

The core idea: requirements in, study pack out

The app is how students receive the material. The real product is the engine behind it. We only type what we need. The engine decides what to search, reads the results, writes the content, checks it, and designs it.

Seedhi baat

Hum log sirf requirement daalenge. Baaki sab, matlab search karna, padhna, likhna, check karna aur design karna, engine khud karega. Aapki 5 hand-made e-books aur 5th sem notebook isi output ka gold standard hain.

CHAPTER 04

LLM, RAG and Agents: what each one does

Knowledge-Vault is an Agentic RAG-based Multi-Agent System. That name has three parts. Each one solves a different problem, so you need to understand all three.

THE BRAIN

LLM

A Large Language Model (Claude, GPT, Gemini, Llama) reads, writes and reasons in language. On its own it can explain anything, but it sometimes makes facts up and does not know your syllabus.

THE MEMORY

RAG

Retrieval-Augmented Generation. Before writing, the system retrieves real documents and the LLM writes only from them, with citations. This is how we stop hallucination.

THE HANDS

Agent

An LLM that can use tools (web search, page reader, calculator) and decide its own next step in a loop: think → act → observe → repeat, until the job is done.

THE COMPANY

Multi-agent

Several agents, each with one job (planner, researcher, writer, verifier), passing work to each other like a team.

CHAPTER 07

A real run, step by step: BCS501 DBMS

Here is what the engine does for one subject, the same subject as the 5th-semester notebook we already made by hand. Follow one topic, "3.3 Normal Forms", all the way through.

  1. We give the requirements

    One command or one form on the admin page.

    kv generate --university AKTU --course "B.Tech CSE" --sem 5 \ --subject "BCS501 DBMS" --syllabus aktu_bcs501.pdf --pyqs papers/bcs501/

  2. Planner builds the topic tree

    Reads the syllabus PDF and returns 5 units and about 25 topics, each with the block types it needs.

    Unit 3 · Dependencies & Normalization 3.1 Functional dependencies, closure, keys [concept, numerical] 3.2 Canonical cover [concept, numerical] 3.3 Normal forms 1NF → BCNF, 4NF [concept, table, numerical, long-answer]

  3. Researcher gathers sources for 3.3

    Writes 6 search queries ("BCNF vs 3NF example", "decompose relation into BCNF lossless"…), opens about 15 pages, keeps the best 6 after scoring and de-duplication, and stores them as embedded chunks.

  4. PYQ Analyser scores the topic

    Normal forms appear in most past papers as a 7 or 10-mark question, so 3.3 gets ★★★ and is flagged "highest scoring part".

  5. Writer drafts the topic

    Retrieves the top chunks and writes: a definition, a 1NF→5NF comparison table, a "find the highest normal form" numerical with steps, a BCNF decomposition numerical, 4 flashcards and 3 MCQs. Each fact carries a citation like [s2]. It imitates the style of our hand-made notebook.

  6. Verifier checks it

    Splits the draft into about 40 small claims and checks each against the sources. It recomputes the candidate keys in the numerical with Python. If one claim is unsupported or a step is wrong, that block goes back to the Writer with the reason.

  7. Verified JSON is saved

    The topic becomes structured data (see Chapter 9) and is stored in the database.

  8. Renderers publish all formats

    The same JSON becomes an e-book chapter, a notebook page section, a flashcard deck and a quiz. After admin review, it appears in the app for students.

Target for the whole subject: under 30 minutes and a small API cost, compared with 2–3 days of manual work. We measure both (Chapter 14).
CHAPTER 08

The verifier loop: how we stop wrong content

The biggest danger with AI-generated study material is a confident mistake. A student who learns a wrong formula loses marks. That is why nothing is published until it passes the Verifier.

CHECK 1

Is every claim supported?

Each small fact must be backed by at least one retrieved source. Unsupported claims are removed or rewritten.

CHECK 2

Is the maths right?

Numericals (closures, keys, sorting steps, complexities) are recomputed with Python and compared with the written answer.

CHECK 3

Is the syllabus covered?

Every subtopic in the plan must appear. Missing ones are sent back for writing.

CHECK 4

Is it original?

Text overlap with any single source is measured, so we never copy a website word for word.

CHAPTER 09

One source, four formats

The Writer does not produce an e-book or a PDF directly. It produces structured content in one JSON format. Separate renderers turn that one source into every format. Write once, publish everywhere.

What one block looks like

// one numerical block inside topic 3.3 (simplified)
{
  "type": "numerical",
  "importance": 3,                        // ★★★ from PYQ Analyser
  "marks": 7,
  "question": "Decompose R(A,B,C,D), F = {AB→C, C→D, D→A} into BCNF.",
  "steps": [
    "Find keys: AB, AC, BD are candidate keys.",
    "C→D violates BCNF (C is not a superkey). Split into (C,D) and (A,B,C).",
    "In (A,B,C), C→A violates BCNF. Split into (C,A) and (B,C)."
  ],
  "answer": "(C,D), (C,A), (B,C)",
  "verified": { "claims_ok": true, "recomputed": true },
  "sources": ["s2", "s5"]
}
Why this matters: consistent design across every subject, progress tracking per topic in the app, and new formats later (audio summary, Hindi edition) without regenerating content.
CHAPTER 10

PYQ intelligence: where the ★ comes from

In our hand-made notebook, we marked important topics with ★ from experience. The engine does it with data: it reads 8–10 years of previous-year papers and measures how often each topic is asked and for how many marks.

  1. Collect papers

    PDFs or photos of past papers. Scanned ones go through OCR.

  2. Extract questions

    The LLM splits each paper into individual questions with their marks.

  3. Group similar questions

    Convert every question to an embedding and cluster them, so "Explain BCNF with example" and "Define BCNF. How is it different from 3NF?" land together.

  4. Map to syllabus topics

    Match each cluster to the nearest topic in the Planner's topic tree.

  5. Score and predict

    Score each topic by frequency, marks and recency. Test predictions on a held-out year: how many of that year's questions did we mark ★?

ML angle

This is the classic machine-learning part of the project: embeddings, clustering (k-means / HDBSCAN), similarity search, and a measurable prediction metric (PYQ hit-rate).

CHAPTER 12

The build process

We build in phases. Each phase ends with something that works and can be demonstrated. The engine comes first, because it is the project's core; the app comes after, because it only delivers what the engine produces.

PhaseWeeksDone when…
1 · Design1–2Literature survey written; content JSON schema agreed; repo and CI set up
2 · Planner + Research3–5Syllabus PDF → topic tree works; each topic gets ≥ 5 ranked sources in the vector DB
3 · Writer + PDF6–8BCS501 DBMS fully generated as a PDF notebook; compared side by side with our hand-made one
4 · Verifier + e-book9–10Feedback loop works; interactive e-book, flashcards and quiz render from the same JSON
5 · PYQ + evaluation11–12★ scores from real papers; coverage, faithfulness and hit-rate measured; ablation done
6 · App + payments13–14Students can sign up, choose a semester, read free content, pay and unlock
7 · Pilot + report15–16Real students use it before exams; results, report and demo ready

Gen-AI-n-ML-solutions/ ├── docs/ # proposal, synopsis, this handbook ├── engine/ # the Knowledge-Vault engine (Python) │ ├── agents/ # planner.py, researcher.py, writer.py, verifier.py │ ├── pyq/ # question extraction, clustering, scoring │ ├── store/ # embeddings + vector DB access │ ├── schema/ # content JSON schema │ ├── renderers/ # ebook/, pdf/, flashcards/, quiz/ │ └── cli.py # "kv generate ..." ├── eval/ # metrics, benchmark, ablation scripts ├── samples/ # our hand-made e-books + notebook (gold standard) ├── app/ # Next.js web app + admin panel └── .github/workflows/ # tests + deploy
Working rules: every feature goes on its own branch and is merged through a pull request reviewed by one teammate. Every agent has unit tests with saved example inputs, so prompt changes can be compared before and after.

CHAPTER 14

Proving it works

Saying "it works" is not enough for evaluators or interviewers. We measure. Our hand-made e-books and notebook are the benchmark the engine is compared against.

MetricQuestion it answersHow we measureTarget
Syllabus coverageDid we miss anything?Subtopics present ÷ subtopics in syllabus≥ 90%
FaithfulnessIs it true?Claims supported by sources ÷ all claims (FActScore / RAGAS)≥ 90%
Numerical accuracyIs the maths right?Correct solutions ÷ all numericals, checked by code and by hand≥ 95%
PYQ hit-rateDoes it help in the exam?Questions in a held-out paper covered by ★ topics≥ 70%
Human preferenceIs it as good as ours?Blind rating by students and faculty, 5-point rubric, generated vs hand-made≥ 4 / 5
Time and costCan it scale?Minutes and ₹ of API use per subject< 30 min
The sentence we want to say at the end

"Our engine generates a complete syllabus-aligned subject in under 30 minutes, with over 90% syllabus coverage and over 90% verified faithfulness, rated close to hand-made material by students." The numbers will be whatever we actually measure.

Risks and how we handle them

RiskWhat we do
Wrong contentRAG grounding, Verifier agent, numericals recomputed by code, human review before paid content goes live

CHAPTER 18

Glossary

TermMeaning in this project
LLMLarge Language Model. The AI that reads and writes text; the brain of every agent.
RAGRetrieval-Augmented Generation. Find sources first, then write only from them.
AgentAn LLM that uses tools and decides its own next step in a loop.
Multi-agent systemSeveral agents with separate roles working together.
EmbeddingA list of numbers that represents the meaning of a text, so similar texts have similar numbers.
Vector databaseA database that finds texts by similar meaning using embeddings (pgvector, ChromaDB).
ChunkA small piece (a few paragraphs) of a source document, stored and retrieved separately.
HallucinationWhen an LLM states something false with confidence.
GroundingTying every generated statement to a real source.
FaithfulnessThe share of generated claims that the sources actually support.
PYQPrevious-year question (paper).
ClusteringGrouping similar items automatically, used here to group similar exam questions.
Content schemaThe fixed JSON structure every topic is stored in.
RendererCode that turns content JSON into a format: HTML, PDF, flashcards, quiz.
Ablation studyRemoving or adding one component at a time to measure its effect.
WebhookA message Razorpay sends to our server when a payment succeeds, so we can unlock content.
